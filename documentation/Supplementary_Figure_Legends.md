# Supplementary figure legends

**Figure S1. MCA dimensionality and geometric fidelity.** (A) Observed eigenvalues and the rank-matched 95th percentiles from within-participant parallel analysis. The leading retained run comprises 13 dimensions. (B) Distance correlation, trustworthiness and neighbor recall for truncated spaces relative to the complete 54-D MCA space. Neighborhood measures use 10 other event-states, explicitly excluding the event itself. Numerically tied distances use a consistent event-identifier order. (C) Cumulative raw, Benzécri-normalized and Greenacre-adjusted inertia. These are different summaries of the same MCA solution. (D) Dimensions retained after omitting each participant and refitting MCA with parallel analysis. The full-sample reference retains 13 dimensions; every omission retains at least 10. The 4-D geometry is the main analysis space selected for macro-organization, rather than a four-dimension parallel-analysis result. Axis ranks start at 1; coordinate names start at Dim0.

**Figure S2. Context- and anchor-conditioned event-state displacement.** (A–D) Fields conditioned on social, physical, internal/simulated and virtual/digital source contexts. (E–H) Fields conditioned on social, static-landmark, goal and dynamic-landmark source anchors. The corresponding source-transition counts are 143, 64, 30, 14, 146, 66, 18 and 15. Six no-anchor transitions are excluded from E–H. Destinations remain unrestricted. All panels share the Dim0 × Dim3 projection, a 25 × 25 grid, overall event-density background, smoothing and support threshold. Displacement sums and source counts use Gaussian smoothing of 0.75 grid cells; arrows are supported where smoothed source count is at least 0.05. Density smoothing uses 1.0 grid cell. Streamlines depict local mean one-step displacement, not observed multistep trajectories. These conditioned fields are descriptive. The pooled observed and exact expected shuffled fields appear in main Figure 2.

**Figure S3. Geometric tradeoffs and basin-state composition.** (A) The 2,030 eligible construction configurations are shown as equally opaque circles; the five retained solutions are slightly larger outlined circles. The horizontal axis shows the fraction of variation in common 4-D coordinates accounted for by basin centroids. The vertical axis shows the lowest basin-mean silhouette, calculated in each solution’s fitting space. It identifies the least-separated basin-state within that configuration; zero is a reference rather than an eligibility cutoff. The 2-D and 4-D silhouettes describe different fitting geometries. Retention also depended on assignment stability, outgoing support and partition diversity, so position in this display does not determine the retained set (Supplement S4; Table S2G). (B) Mean basin occupancy is shown by a horizontal bar, with basin-specific event counts shown by circles on a shared horizontal line. Multipliers identify coincident counts. (C) Basin-mean silhouettes in each solution’s fitting space. Basin labels are local to a solution. Counts and assignment stability do not establish precise estimation of every transition probability.

**Figure S4. Feature–routing correspondence using all 11 active MCA variables.** (A) 2-D 9-Basin. (B) 4-D 10-Basin. Each point represents a unique basin pair and compares feature-profile cosine similarity with one-step routing-profile cosine similarity. Lines are descriptive fits. Primary and secondary mechanism fields remain separate in this all-variable feature definition. Statistical estimates use the existing two-sided common-label permutation tests; these comparisons are not adjusted across basin solutions.

**Figure S5. Representational–successor correspondence in the 2-D 9-Basin solution.** (A) Basin centroids in the Dim0 × Dim3 projection. (B) Complete successor profiles projected onto their first two principal components. (C) Two-dimensional Procrustes alignment of these displays. Filled circles denote representational positions and white-centered circles denote successor positions. Connecting segments show alignment residuals. (D) Pairwise basin-centroid distances in the complete 4-D geometry versus distances between complete successor profiles, across 36 basin pairs. The line is descriptive. The first two successor components retain 39.1% of successor-profile variance; inference concerning complete profiles does not depend on this reduced display. Basin fitting uses two dimensions, whereas the distances in D use four. Colors identify basin-states within this solution. The successor discount is γ = 0.90 (Tables S4A–B).

**Figure S6. Transition-unit, profile-definition and narrative-order controls.** (A–B) Complete successor-profile distance correspondence after collapsing consecutive repeated visits to the same basin, shown for 4-D 10-Basin and 2-D 9-Basin, respectively. The comparison changes the transition unit from consecutive event-states to successive basin visits. (C) Representational correspondence for one-step routing, identity plus discounted one-step routing, complete successor profiles, and successor profiles with the identity term removed. Color denotes Pearson r. Asterisks denote nominal two-sided common-label permutation p values: *p < .05, **p < .01, ***p < .001. They do not indicate a correction across the displayed cells. (D) Correspondence for narrated and reversed order, compared with the central 95% of within-participant shuffled-order results. The gray ranges describe the shuffle distribution, not confidence intervals around the observed estimates; zero marks no linear correlation. Sequence reversal preserves adjacency while changing direction. These controls do not by themselves establish a unique multistep contribution or forward-direction specificity. Distances use the 4-D geometry and successor-based comparisons use γ = 0.90. Basin-visit, profile-definition, and narrative-order results are reported in Tables S4D, S4F, and S4E, respectively.
